Research question

How do SEB skills develop, and which skills do people want to change?

Focus: Development, change goals, and beliefs about the malleability of social, emotional, and behavioral (SEB) skills.

Outputs: Studies on age-related differences and on goals for changing skills and personality traits.

Related publications Key findings

The project

SEB skills describe what people are capable of doing when situations require it, including managing goals, cooperating, engaging socially, innovating, and regulating emotions.

This research programme examines how these skills differ across age groups and how people think about developing them. It brings together developmental psychology, personality research, and motivation research.

Research approach

The studies examine three complementary perspectives:

  • Development: Age- and gender-related patterns in SEB skills, particularly during adolescence.
  • Beliefs about change: How adolescents, parents, teachers, and other professionals view the feasibility and desirability of changing skills and traits.
  • Change goals: Which characteristics people want to develop, and how skill-based versus trait-based framing relates to motivation.

Key findings

  • Skills show distinct age-related patterns. Research with adolescents identifies differences across ages and genders. These comparisons describe age-related variation; they do not, by themselves, establish how a particular individual changes over time (Feraco and Meneghetti 2023).
  • Skills and traits elicit different change goals. Research with young adults finds differences in the goals people report for SEB skills and their corresponding personality traits (Feraco, Hudson, and Soto 2025).
  • Beliefs matter for personal development. The broader programme also examines how students, parents, and teachers evaluate the possibility of change and which areas they prioritize.

Next questions

The aim is to understand when and how SEB skill development can be supported. Preferences for changing a skill should be distinguished from evidence that a particular intervention changes it effectively.
